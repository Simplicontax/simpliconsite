Record keeping

How long should you keep records?

Generally, you must keep records that support an item of income, deduction or credit shown on your tax return until the period of limitations for that return runs out.

You must keep records as long as they may be needed for the administration of any provision of the Internal Revenue Code. Generally, this is 3 years from the date of filing the tax return unless the taxpayer filed a fraudulent tax return or under-reported income.

The IRS does not require you to keep records in a particular way. Keep them in a manner that allows you and the IRS to determine your correct tax.

You can use your checkbook to keep a record of income and expenses. Keep documents such as receipts and sales slips that help prove a deduction.

To summarize

  • Keep tax records for at least 3 years from the filing date.
  • Keep documents supporting income, deductions and credits.
  • Keep W-2 records until you begin receiving Social Security benefits.
  • The IRS may request supporting documents within the applicable period of limitations.
  • Keep proof of payment for itemized deductions, including receipts, cancelled checks and bank or credit-card statements.
  • If expenses cannot be substantiated, the IRS may disallow the related deduction and assess additional tax.
